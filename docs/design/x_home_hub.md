# Isopod-X Home Hub

X has a tranquil home-base room that functions as a compact recovery and loadout hub between stages.

## Required interactions

- **Rest:** a bed built into the left side of the shell-like room. Resting can restore X and provide a calm visual break between combat stages.
- **Save:** a computer terminal on a desk in the center-right portion of the room. This is the primary save interaction.
- **Change weapons:** a display case on the right side of the room. At the beginning of the game it contains only X's initial living Springtail Peashooter. Additional weapons or weapon forms can populate this area later.

## Art direction

The architecture should vaguely resemble the **inside of an isopod carapace**. Curved overlapping shell ribs form the walls and ceiling, with repeated segmented arches, organic conduits, and shell-like structural supports.

The visual language may borrow from biomechanical surrealism: fused organic and mechanical forms, ribbed structures, inset conduits, rounded apertures, and sculptural machinery. The mood must remain **tranquil, safe, domestic, and contemplative**, not horror-oriented.

Suggested palette:

- charcoal and weathered shell gray architecture
- muted violet and blue ambient light
- warm amber bedside lighting
- soft purple textiles and rugs
- restrained cyan computer illumination

## Gameplay readability

The three main stations should be visually separated and readable in a side-scrolling room:

1. Bed on the left
2. X / open floor space near center
3. Save computer on desk to the right of center
4. Weapon display at far right

No station should visually compete with X. Interaction points should eventually receive clear game-specific prompts or highlights rather than relying only on environmental detail.

## Canonical visual notes

The current reference shows X using the Series 1 toy-inspired armored design: segmented silver shell, blue armor, red X accents, purple limb sections, large boots, large eyes, antennae, and tusk-like mandibles.
