# Isopod-X Toy Line — Scale, Proportions, and Construction Language

## Purpose

This document defines the dimensional language for the Isopod-X collectible micro-beast toy line. The goal is to capture the compact, exaggerated feel of late-1980s approximately 2-inch creature figures while keeping the designs original to Isopod-X.

These values are **design targets**, not claims of exact factory measurements for every vintage figure. Individual characters may deviate when horns, antennae, wings, mandibles, tails, or other anatomy require it.

## Overall Scale

- Nominal figure height: **50.8 mm / 2.0 in** from foot sole to top of the main head mass.
- Practical character range: **48–57 mm** depending on species and silhouette.
- Antennae, horns, crests, wings, tails, and similar appendages may extend beyond the nominal height.
- Figures should read as **compact mini-figures**, not 3.75-inch or 4-inch modern action figures.
- The body should feel dense and toy-like, with large masses and minimal empty space.

## Core Proportion Targets

Use these as starting targets for front-view design:

- Head: **28–32%** of total body height.
- Torso + pelvis: **32–35%** of total body height.
- Legs: **35–40%** of total body height.
- Shoulder width: approximately **65–70%** of total body height.
- Head width: approximately **45–55%** of total body height for broad characters.
- Torso width: approximately **50–65%** of total body height.
- Foot length: approximately **18–24%** of total body height.
- Hand/claw mass: approximately **12–18%** of body height where anatomically appropriate.

The ratios are deliberately exaggerated. Characters should not use realistic human heroic proportions.

## Head Language

- Heads are oversized and visually dominant.
- Favor one strong animal/invertebrate identity cue per head: mandibles, rostrum, tusks, eye stalks, shell brow, antennae, etc.
- Eyes should be large enough to read clearly at 2-inch scale.
- Avoid narrow human skull proportions.
- Keep fragile protrusions thick enough for handling and FDM printing when adapted to printable models.

## Torso Language

- Torso is short, broad, and compact.
- Minimal waist taper.
- Chest armor should be composed of a few large readable plates rather than many tiny sci-fi panels.
- Species anatomy should shape the torso silhouette: shell dome, thorax wedge, shield carapace, segmented abdomen, etc.
- The body should remain visually understandable when viewed as a black silhouette.

## Limb Language

- Arms are short and thick.
- Forearms may be oversized when they carry the character's visual gimmick.
- Hands, claws, pincers, clubs, hooks, or blades should be simplified into large toy-readable forms.
- Legs are short, sturdy, and usually wider than realistic anatomy would suggest.
- Feet should be oversized enough to create a stable standing base.
- Avoid long superhero-style thighs, narrow calves, or realistic human musculature.

## Articulation Language

For the strongest vintage micro-beast feel:

- Default articulation: **shoulder rotation only**.
- Arms should visually read as separate molded parts attached at the shoulders.
- Head, torso, pelvis, and legs should read as one primary body mass unless a specific production version requires more pieces.
- Do not default to modern collector articulation such as visible ball-jointed elbows, knees, wrists, ankles, butterfly shoulders, or segmented ab crunches.
- More advanced articulation may be used for special printable or premium versions, but it should not define the visual language of the standard Series 1 figures.

## Silhouette Rules

Each Series 1 figure should remain recognizable when reduced to a flat silhouette.

Prioritize:

1. Distinct head shape.
2. Distinct arm/weapon anatomy.
3. Distinct torso mass.
4. Distinct stance and foot shape.
5. One memorable species-specific appendage or feature.

Avoid solving character differences through surface detailing alone.

## Surface Detail Density

- Use broad armor plates and deep, readable grooves.
- Limit small rivets, screws, panel lines, and greebles.
- Surface details should survive reduction to a roughly 50 mm physical figure.
- Favor sculpted texture and bold plate boundaries over modern hard-surface complexity.
- Paint regions should be large and easy to identify.

## Standard 50.8 mm Design Template

For a nominal **50.8 mm** figure, a useful starting breakdown is:

| Region | Target range |
|---|---:|
| Head height | 14–16 mm |
| Torso + pelvis | 16–18 mm |
| Leg region | 18–20 mm |
| Shoulder width | 33–36 mm |
| Typical torso width | 25–33 mm |
| Typical foot length | 9–12 mm |

These ranges overlap intentionally because the line should prioritize character silhouette over strict uniform anatomy.

## Character-Class Variations

### Heavy / Tank
Examples: Isopod-X, Weta-type bruisers, shield-backed creatures.

- Height: 48–53 mm.
- Wider shoulders and torso.
- Head may approach 32–35% of height.
- Shorter legs.
- Larger feet and hands.

### Assassin / Speed
Examples: Needle Strike, mantis-type fighters.

- Height: 52–57 mm before antennae or weapons.
- Narrower torso.
- Slightly longer limbs allowed.
- Still keep the head oversized and the legs chunky enough to remain in the same toy family.
- Do not drift into 7–8-head-tall superhero anatomy.

### Aerial
Examples: cicada, dobsonfly, mantis.

- Body remains within standard scale.
- Wings may extend the silhouette dramatically but should fold close to the body for a standard toy pose.
- Wing thickness and connection points should be robust in printable adaptations.

### Aquatic / Shell-Based
Examples: mantis shrimp, diving beetle, triops-like designs.

- May use wider carapaces, stalk eyes, paddle limbs, or club arms.
- Keep the body compact even when appendages are extreme.

## Needle Strike Target

Needle Strike should use this dimensional language as follows:

- Main body height: **approximately 52–55 mm**.
- Large assassin-bug head with dominant eyes and long rostrum.
- Head approximately **30%** of main body height.
- Narrow but still chunky thorax.
- Short, strong legs with large feet.
- Arms simpler and shorter than a modern action figure.
- Broad shoulder swivel joints only for the standard version.
- Wings simplified into a small number of thick molded panels close to the back.
- The rostrum provides the primary vertical/forward silhouette instead of oversized handheld weapons.
- Armor should use large molded areas with minimal greebling.

## Isopod-X Target

Isopod-X should sit near the heavier end of the range:

- Main body height: **approximately 50–53 mm**.
- Broad shell/head mass.
- Width emphasized over height.
- Oversized boots and claw hands.
- Segmented shell plates are a primary silhouette feature.
- Compact torso with little or no human-style waist taper.
- Standard figure should visually remain a small armored isopod creature, not a human in insect armor.

## FDM / Printable Adaptation Notes

When translating the line to printable figures:

- Preserve the same external proportions even if internal engineering changes.
- Minimum fragile feature thickness target: **1.5–2.0 mm** where practical.
- Thicken antennae, rostrums, claws, mandibles, and wing roots.
- Flatten or enlarge feet as needed for standing stability.
- Mechanical clearances are a separate engineering concern and should not distort the exterior character ratios.
- A premium articulated print version may use ball joints or pins internally, but the visible silhouette should still resemble the compact standard toy.

## Visual North Star

The line should feel like a shelf of tiny, dense creature warriors with wildly different silhouettes but a shared physical language:

- approximately 2 inches tall,
- oversized animal/invertebrate heads,
- short broad torsos,
- thick limbs,
- huge feet,
- simple articulation,
- bold armor masses,
- minimal fine greebling,
- immediate species readability.

If a design starts to look like a modern 6-inch superhero figure scaled downward, it has drifted away from the target.
